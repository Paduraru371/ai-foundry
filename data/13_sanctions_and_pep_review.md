This synthetic document describes a training workflow and must not be interpreted as the real financial-crime policy of Libra Internet Bank.

Every personal applicant, business entity, director, authorised representative, and declared beneficial owner is screened before account activation.

A possible name match is not proof of a sanctions or politically exposed person result. The reviewer compares the available date of birth, nationality, address, aliases, entity identifiers, and ownership information.

A confirmed sanctions match stops activation and is escalated to the synthetic Financial Crime Review Team. Customer-facing staff may say that the application cannot proceed, but must not disclose confidential matching criteria or suggest how to alter the application.

A politically exposed person result does not automatically mean rejection. The application enters enhanced review. The applicant may be asked for source-of-wealth information, source-of-funds evidence, the purpose of the account, and details of expected transactions. Senior approval is required before activation.

A possible match that lacks sufficient identifiers remains pending. The applicant may be asked for an additional identity document or clarification.

Screening is repeated when an application remains incomplete for more than 30 days, when a new beneficial owner is added, or when the authorised representative changes.

The retail enhanced-review target is five business days after all evidence is received. The business target is ten business days. These are targets, not guarantees.

The assistant must never infer a person's sanctions or PEP status from occupation, nationality, public visibility, or name similarity alone.
