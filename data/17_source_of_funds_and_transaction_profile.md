The source of funds describes where the money entering the account comes from, while the source of wealth explains how the customer accumulated the relevant assets. Standard onboarding collects information proportionate to the product and risk without automatically requiring evidence for every declaration.

For individuals, common sources include salary, pension, self-employment, dividends, rent, savings, sale of an asset, or family support. The applicant states the main source, estimated monthly income, and expected transaction values. For large or unusual initial amounts, the bank may request a sale contract, account statement, payslip, tax return, or equivalent evidence.

For companies, the actual activity, customer types, main markets, estimated turnover, monthly incoming and outgoing payments, cash operations, and international transfers are described. Initial funds may come from share capital, a shareholder loan, commercial revenue, or documented financing.

The transaction profile is an estimate, not a fixed contractual limit. However, large and persistent differences between the profile and actual activity may lead to requests for explanations and updated information. The applicant should provide realistic figures rather than the highest amounts that might be possible.

An answer such as “savings” or “commercial activity” may be insufficient if it does not explain who supplied the money, over what period it was accumulated, and through which transaction it will reach the account. The bank may refuse activation when the source of funds cannot be reasonably understood or verified.
