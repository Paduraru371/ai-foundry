A beneficial owner is the individual who ultimately owns or controls a legal-entity customer or on whose behalf the relationship is conducted. Identification is not limited to the name recorded in a register. The applicant must describe the complete ownership structure and provide enough evidence to verify every level.

In the standard business flow, every individual who directly or indirectly holds more than 20% of the capital or voting rights must be declared. Indirect ownership is calculated along the ownership chain. For example, if an individual owns 60% of a company that owns 40% of the customer, the indirect ownership is 24%.

Control may also exist without exceeding the percentage threshold. The right to appoint management, shareholder agreements, veto rights, or control exercised by other means must be declared. If no individual can be identified through ownership or control, the senior managing official or officials are documented, without this replacing the investigation of the structure.

For each beneficial owner, the bank collects the name, date of birth, citizenship, country of residence, address, nature of control, and ownership percentage. It may request identity documents, organisation charts, shareholder registers, official extracts, or signed declarations.

Differences between the customer's declaration, public registers, and company documents must be clarified before the account is activated. An incomplete declaration, refusal to identify the relevant individuals, or use of unexplained intermediary entities may result in rejection and further compliance measures.
