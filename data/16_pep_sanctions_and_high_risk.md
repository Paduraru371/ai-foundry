During onboarding, the applicant declares whether they are a politically exposed person, a family member, or a close associate of such a person. The declaration is checked against independent sources. Politically exposed person status does not automatically result in rejection, but it requires additional review, establishment of the source of wealth and source of funds, and approval at the level required by the risk policy.

Sanctions screening applies to the applicant, representatives, beneficial owners, and other relevant individuals. A name match is analysed using the date of birth, citizenship, address, and other identifiers. The customer must not be told that a review is delayed because of a specific possible match when such disclosure would interfere with the bank's legal obligations.

High risk may arise from jurisdictions, economic activities, ownership structures, intensive cash use, requested products, remote channels, or inconsistencies in the information. The assessment considers the combination of factors rather than one answer in isolation.

For a high-risk case, the bank may request evidence concerning income, wealth, commercial contracts, customers and suppliers, beneficial owners, the reason for relevant jurisdictions, and the path of the initial funds. The bank may restrict the initial configuration, delay activation, or refuse the relationship when the risk cannot be managed.

The applicant must answer fully and correctly. Hiding PEP status, a person in the control structure, or a connection with a relevant jurisdiction is treated as a significant inconsistency and may result in rejection.
